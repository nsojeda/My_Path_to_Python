## Integers and Floats

Python represents number using two different data types
- integers or int
    - integers or whole numbers 
- floats 
    - Floats are numbers with decimals

```python

num1 = 3.14

print(num)
print(num1)
```

## type( )

Python has a function  - type( ) - to find the type of variable 
You just need to pass the variable as a argument


> [!NOTE]
> **Order of operations** 
> is the standard set of rules for evaluating mathematical expressions so everyone gets the same answer.
> 
> The Rules (PEMDAS / BODMAS)
> 
> 1. **P**arentheses / **B**rackets — solve innermost first
> 2. **E**xponents / **O**rders — powers and roots
> 3. **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision — left to right
> 4. **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction — left to right
> 
> > Multiplication does not come before division; they have equal priority and are done in the order they appear from left to right. Same with addition and subtraction.
> 
> **Example**
> > 
> 3 + 6 × (5 + 4) ÷ 3² − 1
> = 3 + 6 × 9 ÷ 3² − 1      # parentheses
> = 3 + 6 × 9 ÷ 9 − 1       # exponent
> = 3 + 54 ÷ 9 − 1          # multiplication (left to right)
> = 3 + 6 − 1               # division
> = 9 − 1                   # addition
> = 8                       # subtraction
>

> 
> The PEMDAS/BODMAS framework is based on standard mathematical conventions established by the **International Organization for Standardization (ISO 80000-2)** and widely adopted in mathematics education globally.
